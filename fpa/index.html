About FPA

Fountainhead Preschool Adajan

Fountainhead Preschool Adajan (FPA) brings the renowned Fountainhead early years experience to the heart of Adajan, Surat. With the same IB PYP-aligned philosophy that underpins FSK and all FS Group campuses, FPA is the ideal starting point for a child's lifelong love of learning.

At FPA, every child's curiosity is celebrated. Our warm, nurturing environment is designed to help young learners discover who they are, how they express themselves, and how the world works — through inquiry, play, and collaboration.

What a Day at FPA Looks Like

Mornings begin with an inquiry provocation — a question, an object, a problem to explore. Children move through planned and child-led activities, spending time outdoors, in the arts corner, in the library, and in conversation with each other and their teachers. No two days look exactly alike — and that's the point.

Safeguarding Policy

Child Protection Policy

The safety and wellbeing of every child at Fountainhead Preschool Adajan is our highest priority. We are committed to creating a safe, secure, and nurturing environment where every child can flourish.

Key Commitments

  • All staff undergo mandatory Child Protection training
  • A designated Child Protection Officer (CPO) is available at all times
  • Any concerns about a child's welfare are reported immediately and investigated
  • We follow POCSO (Protection of Children from Sexual Offences) Act guidelines
  • All allegations are treated seriously, impartially, and confidentially

Reporting Concerns

If you have any concerns about a child's safety, please contact us at adajan@fountainheadschools.org immediately. All concerns will be acted upon within 24 hours.

Academic Policy

Academic Integrity Policy

FPA is committed to authentic, honest learning from the earliest age. We value original thinking, genuine curiosity, and the courage to try. Every child's work is their own — and that ownership is celebrated.

Key Principles

  • Authentic effort and original expression are celebrated at all stages
  • Children learn to share ideas openly and attribute them to their source
  • Teachers guide collaborative activities to ensure learning rather than copying

Language Policy

Language is central to all learning at FPA. We are committed to multilingualism and believe that all adults in the school are language role models.

Language of Instruction

  • English is the primary language of instruction
  • Mother tongue language support is provided and celebrated
  • Children are encouraged to maintain and develop their home language
  • EAL (English as an Additional Language) support is available
Administrative Policy

Admission Policy

FPA admits children through a welcoming, transparent process. We admit children aged 3–6 to our Early Years programme.

Eligibility

  • Children must be aged 3 years or above at the time of admission
  • A warm, play-based interaction is part of the process
  • We welcome children from all backgrounds

Documents Required

  • Birth certificate / Age proof
  • Passport-size photographs
  • Residential address proof
  • Aadhar card (child and parents)
Health Policy

Health Exclusion Policy

To protect the health of all children and staff, FPA follows a Health Exclusion Policy specifying when children should be kept home and when they may return.

When to Keep Your Child at Home

  • Fever (above 100°F / 37.8°C) — child must be fever-free for 24 hours before returning
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea — must be symptom-free for 24 hours
  • Chickenpox — until all blisters have crusted over
  • Pink eye (Conjunctivitis) — until treatment has begun and discharge has stopped
Partnership Policy

Parent Role in Education

FPA believes that a child's early years are shaped most powerfully by the partnership between home and school. Parents are our most important partners in learning.

How Parents Can Help

  • Attend orientation sessions to understand the FPA learning approach
  • Stay curious with your child — ask open questions, not just "what did you do today?"
  • Build a reading culture at home from an early age
  • Encourage independence in small daily tasks
  • Avoid over-scheduling — children need unstructured time to simply be
  • Participate in school events, exhibitions, and parent conferences

Demystifying IB

Common Myths About IB Education

Myth

IB is only for gifted students

The IB is designed for all learners. Inquiry works for every child — it does not sort them into gifted and not gifted, it asks all of them to think.

Myth

IB students cannot do engineering or medicine

IB DP is accepted by universities for STEM, medicine, law, management — in India and worldwide. The subject combination is what matters, not the board.

Myth

IB means there are no exams

IB has rigorous external assessments. DP students sit comprehensive written exams marked by IB examiners worldwide — it is among the most respected qualifications globally.

Myth

IB does not prepare students for competitive exams

IB develops deep analytical thinking and strong academic foundations. FSK students successfully clear JEE, NEET, SAT, and other competitive examinations. Coaching for competitive exams is not discouraged — only tuition from former FS teachers is restricted.

Academic Policy

Academic Integrity Policy

Fountainhead School is committed to academic integrity — the honest, authentic, and responsible production of work. Academic integrity is a fundamental value of an IB education and applies to all students across all programmes.

Key Principles

  • All submitted work must be the student's own authentic work
  • Proper citation and referencing of all sources is mandatory
  • Collaboration is encouraged but must be clearly distinguished from collusion
  • Plagiarism, malpractice, and misconduct will be investigated and addressed
  • Students must sign an Academic Integrity Declaration for all major assessments

Consequences of Malpractice

  • First instance: Formal warning, counselling session, parent notification
  • Second instance: Zero on the assignment, parental meeting with Head of School
  • Third instance / Exam malpractice: Reported to IB; potential cancellation of results

Food Policy & Bus Food Policy

Bus Food Policy

Objective: To allow students to eat food (as per food policy) in the bus but with expected behaviour — no littering and spreading dirt.

Applicable to: All students.

Value guiding policy: Excellence — Always implement practices that continue to produce valued outcomes.

Rationale: We have received feedback from students and a few teachers that the students get hungry after school and therefore wanted permission to be allowed to eat in the bus during travel time.

Policy Guidelines

We have considered the suggestion and decided to lift the ban of eating in the bus. The students will have to follow the rules mentioned below to maintain cleanliness and discipline in the bus.

  • Only healthy foods like fruits, peanuts, brown bread items will be allowed in the bus
  • All junk food like wafers, biscuits, white bread items, chocolates, and liquid items will NOT be allowed
  • No sharing of food or passing of dabba in bus
  • No throwing of food at each other or throwing anything out of the window
  • If anything is dropped or spilled, the student responsible has to clean the same

Consequences

Didis will have full authority in the bus to:

  • Immediately report misbehaviour, throwing food, etc. to the Operation Manager and Home Room Teacher
  • Confiscate prohibited items
  • 1st Warning (by driver/didi): Student will not be allowed to eat in the bus for that journey
  • 2nd Warning: Student will be referred to the Principal
  • 3rd Warning: Parents will be called and disciplinary action will be taken

Academic Policy

No Tuition Policy

Fountainhead School strongly discourages students from taking private tuitions from former or current Fountainhead teachers. The school believes that its own instruction is sufficient for students to achieve their academic goals, and that tuition creates an unhealthy dependence that undermines the learning process.

Policy Guidelines

  • Students going to former Fountainhead teachers for tuition face detention if continued after the policy start date
  • Continued violation results in being barred from assessments and examinations
  • Grade 10 and Grade 12 students were exempted for the then-current academic year only
  • Grades 7—11 were given time until end of Term 1 to phase out existing tuitions

Important Clarification

Coaching for competitive examinations such as NEET, JEE, SAT, GMAT, GRE, etc. is NOT discouraged. Only academic tuitions from former or current FS teachers are restricted under this policy.

The School's Offer

The school offers optional, paid practice sessions (for subjects like Mathematics) conducted by current FS teachers during or after school hours. These are available to all students who wish additional guided practice in a structured environment.

Health Policy

Health Exclusion Policy

To protect the health of all students and staff, FSK follows a Health Exclusion Policy that specifies when students should be kept home and when they may return to school.

When to Keep Your Child at Home

  • Fever (temperature above 100—F / 37.8—C) — child must be fever-free for 24 hours before returning
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea — must be symptom-free for 24 hours
  • Chickenpox — until all blisters have crusted over (usually 7 days)
  • Pink eye (Conjunctivitis) — until treatment has begun and discharge has stopped
  • Suspected COVID-19 / flu — follow current government and school guidelines

Behaviour Policy

Discipline Policy & Anti-Bullying Policy

What is Bullying?

Bullying is the deliberate, conscious desire to hurt, threaten, or frighten someone. It can be physical, verbal, emotional, or cyberbullying. It is repeated behaviour — not a one-off incident.

Possible Signs of Bullying

A student might have been bullied if he/she shows some of the following signs. Adults should be aware of these possible signs and investigate if a student:

  • Doesn't want to get on the school bus; begs to be driven to school
  • Changes their usual routine
  • Becomes withdrawn, anxious, or lacking in confidence
  • Starts stammering
  • Attempts or threatens suicide or runs away
  • Cries themselves to sleep at night or has nightmares / bed wetting
  • Feels ill in the morning
  • Begins to do poorly in school work
  • Comes home with clothes torn or books damaged
  • Has possessions end up "missing"
  • Asks for money or starts stealing money (to pay bully)
  • Has unexplained cuts or bruises
  • Comes home starving (money / lunch has been stolen)
  • Becomes aggressive, disruptive or unreasonable
  • Is bullying other students or siblings
  • Stops eating
  • Is frightened to say what's wrong
  • Gives improbable excuses for any of the above

These signs and behaviour could indicate other problems, but bullying should be considered a possibility and should be investigated.

Why Do People Bully?

  1. People bully to avoid facing up to their inadequacy and doing something about it
  2. A person also bullies others because they may think they are much superior to them
  3. Some people bully others because they desperately want something they cannot achieve — something which other individuals have, such as study notes or an electronic gadget
  4. To reduce their fear of being seen for what they are — a weak, inadequate and often incompetent individual
  5. Many people bully in order to attract attention from others

Who Do Bullies Target?

  • People who don't stand up for themselves
  • People who are quiet and won't tell on them
  • People who are isolated or have few friends
  • People who get upset or react easily

What Can You Do If You Are Being Bullied?

  • Tell a trusted adult — a teacher, counsellor, or parent — immediately
  • Avoid the bully when possible; travel with friends
  • Stand up calmly and confidently — bullies often back off when not given a reaction
  • Do not fight back physically — this escalates the situation

What Can You Do If You Witness Bullying?

  • Do not ignore it — report it to an adult immediately
  • Support the victim; let them know they are not alone
  • Do not join in or laugh — this encourages the bully
  • Lead the victim away from the situation if it is safe to do so

Anti-Bullying Policy — Implementation

The anti-bullying policy is implemented when:

  • Abusive words are used
  • Hitting or pushing in dangerous places
  • A group bullies an individual

Terminology: Complainer (the victim) — Faultier (the bully) — Arbiter (the teacher/authority handling the case).

Process: Report within 2 working days ? HRT records in Behavioural Observation Form ? Joint meeting held ? Evidence assessed ? Consequences applied as per Discipline Policy.

Steps to Be Taken in School

  • Post anti-bullying rules visibly in every classroom
  • Train students regularly on recognising and responding to bullying
  • Circle time follow-ups for bully and victim
  • Anti-bullying pledge banner in common areas
  • Class unity activities to build community and belonging
  • Anonymous notes box for confidential reporting
  • Take all complaints seriously and investigate promptly
  • Anti-bullying campaign events across the school year
  • Involve parents at every stage of the process

Consequences of Bullying

Bullying has a destructive impact on all parties — bully, victim, and bystander. It can lead to self-doubt, anxiety, depression, and in severe cases, self-harm. Consequences for bullying at FSK range from detention and parental meetings to suspension and expulsion for repeated or serious incidents, in line with the Discipline Policy.

Partnership Policy

Parent Role in Education

The school believes that a child's education is best when school and parents work in genuine partnership. This policy outlines what we expect from parents as active participants in their child's learning.

School Activities & Expectations

  • Attend all Parent Orientation sessions to stay current with each grade's expectations
  • Attend Parent-Teacher Conferences (PTCs) and Student-Led Conferences (SLCs) — these are the primary occasions to understand your child's overall progress
  • Participate in school events: class presentations, role plays, exhibitions, sports day, annual day
  • Volunteer for school activities such as field trips, excursions, and camps
  • Follow school rules and regulations in intent and spirit
  • Support your child to do self-study and homework — not do it for them
  • Take an active interest in what your child is learning at school by asking open questions, not nagging
  • Help with surveys, projects, and research by guiding — not outsourcing or completing them
  • Support student-led projects: PYP Exhibition, Community Project, Personal Project

With Your Child at Home

  • Build a reading culture — read aloud, make good books available, encourage reading buddies
  • Encourage your child to speak and express themselves in their own time and way
  • Foster independence in learning habits and daily routine
  • Build the relationship on mutual trust and respect — not fear
  • Get to know your child's friends, in school and outside
  • Expose children to a variety of experiences and places beyond malls and restaurants
  • Have reasonable expectations — do not ask children to fulfil unrealised parental ambitions
  • Do not over-schedule; children need unstructured play and time to simply be

Family Culture

  • Values: Role-model and discuss integrity, respect, commitment, and tolerance
  • Balanced routines: Ensure avenues for physical, mental, and socio-emotional development alongside academics

Consequences: There are no enforced consequences for parents. The natural consequences of an unsupported home environment fall on the child's development — which is reason enough to take this role seriously.
